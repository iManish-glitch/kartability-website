Outdoor adaptive go-kartingAccess, safety and fun for more people

Why we want to do this

We want everyone to have a chance to join in.

Outdoor go-karting is exciting—but standard karts and facilities can create barriers for people with disabilities. Kartability aims to reduce those barriers through adaptive equipment, accessible routes to and around the track, and thoughtful support.

Our vision is to create a welcoming outdoor recreational experience in the South Shore and Annapolis Valley where friends and families can take part together, with dignity and as much independence as possible.

What we are planning

Small changes can make a big difference.

Every person’s needs are different. These are the features we plan to explore for our track with participants and qualified equipment specialists.

↔

Adaptive controls

Hand-operated controls and suitable steering adaptations for participants who may not be able to use standard pedals, assessed for safe use with the kart.

Comfort & transfer support

Adjustable seats, transfer supports, and properly fitted safety harnesses to help accommodate different mobility and positioning needs.

Clear communication

Built-in speakers in helmets and clear instructions to help participants hear guidance during the activity.

Accessible spaces

Accessible paths where needed, firm and even routes to and around the outdoor track, and universal traffic signals with clear, distinguishable cues to support access and make track directions easier to understand.

04 / ACCESS

Planned features are subject to technical assessment, site suitability, funding, and safety approval. Equipment suitability will be assessed individually.

The outdoor setup

Making the outdoor track easier to access.

The project is focused on an outdoor adaptive go-karting experience. Access planning will consider the route from parking and check-in areas to the track, accessible ramps where there are changes in level, and safe, firm paths around the site.

Universal traffic signals are intended to provide cues that are easier to distinguish than colour alone. The final layout and equipment will depend on the facility, specialist advice, and safety requirements.

Safety matters

We need to get the safety details right.

Accessibility and safety need to work together. Adaptive equipment must be suitable for the vehicle, installed or approved by qualified specialists, and checked before use.

  • Equipment compatibility and inspection
  • Staff and volunteer preparation
  • Clear instructions and emergency procedures
  • Respectful discussion of individual support needs

We will share confirmed operating requirements and safety information as the project develops. No sessions should be booked until the program is operational.

Our community

Planned for Western Nova Scotia.

Our outdoor adaptive go-karting project is focused on the South Shore and Annapolis Valley. We hope to work alongside local families, recreation providers, disability-serving organizations, automotive businesses, and municipal partners to make the idea a reality.

Community input will help us understand what people need, which adaptations matter most, and how to make the experience welcoming from arrival to the final lap.
